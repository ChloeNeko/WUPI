# WUPI Roleplay Features

A simple guide to what WUPI can do for stories and roleplay.

WUPI is part storyteller, part game master. She runs a living
world that changes on its own, plays fair, and never just hands
you a win for free.

This guide explains everything in plain words. No tech knowledge
needed.

## 1. How WUPI Tells a Story

Behind every turn, two helpers work together. You only ever see
one of them, but both are always working.

THE RULE-KEEPER (works quietly, behind the scenes)
- It rolls the dice.
- It tracks the time of day.
- It moves you around the map.
- It decides if your actions succeed or fail.
- You never see its work directly.

THE STORYTELLER (the words you actually read)
- It writes what happens on screen.
- It only describes what the Rule-Keeper says happened.
- It cannot cheat, give you free wins, or change an outcome.

Because these two jobs are split apart, no single part of WUPI
can bend the rules to be nice to you. The storyteller can't
secretly make you win, because it isn't allowed to decide who
wins. The world plays fair.

## 2. Starting a Story (Make One or Bring Your Own)

When you click "New Game," the Game Master greets you on a calm,
dark backdrop and offers a choice:

  - Make a new character from scratch, OR
  - Import a character you already own.

MAKE A NEW CHARACTER - The Interview
The Game Master asks you questions, one at a time, in a warm and
atmospheric way. Who are you? Where have you been? What haunts
you?

While you talk, a quiet helper listens to everything you say and
pulls out the important facts. As you answer, you'll watch your
character come together at the top of the screen, piece by piece.
Your name, your background, the places that matter, the people in
your life.

IMPORT A CHARACTER YOU ALREADY OWN
Have a character card file from another AI chat app? You can drag
and drop it right onto the screen (or pick it from a folder).
WUPI understands files from lots of different apps - picture
files with the character info hidden inside, plain text files of
character data, or lore books (big collections of world details).

Drop the file in and WUPI will figure out what's inside and turn
it into a clean, readable character card. You'll see a live
preview as it works. If the original file was messy or written in
a clunky shorthand, WUPI neatly rewrites it into proper story
prose. If it was already nicely written, it keeps your words
faithfully.

Any lore book entries that come with the card get tucked away
into Wupi's library (see section 13) so they show up when they're
relevant. Then you get the same friendly refinement chat as the
"make from scratch" path, so you can tweak anything before you
begin.

FINISHING UP
When you click "Begin," the screen fades to black, your character
is saved, the world is set up, and you step onto the stage. The
conversation itself is never saved. Your character card is the
only thing that remains.

QUICK PLAY
A faster way in. Answer four quick questions and you'll be
playing in about 24 seconds.

## 3. A World That Lives and Breathes

This is what makes WUPI's worlds feel alive instead of just
reactive to you.

THE WORLD REMEMBERS REAL FACTS
Every turn, WUPI quietly reviews what's actually true right now:

- What time of day is it?
- What's the weather doing?
- Where am I, and where can I go from here?
- Who is in the room with me?
- What rumors are going around?

The storyteller has to obey these facts. If a character isn't
listed as being in the room, they cannot speak. They're simply
not there.

THE WORLD MOVES WITHOUT YOU
When enough time passes in the story, the world updates itself
behind the scenes. Weather shifts. Rumors spread. Tasks get
done (or fail). The world has its own life and its own plans,
whether you're watching or not.

CHARACTERS STAY THEMSELVES
Once a character's core identity is set, it can't slowly drift
into someone else over a long session. The gruff blacksmith
stays gruff. The clever thief stays clever. Canon is locked.

## 4. The Rules Play Fair (No Free Wins)

This is the heart of WUPI. The computer rolls the dice. The
storyteller never gets to decide an outcome. There are a few
silent "referees" that run before you see any words.

SKILL CHECKS
When you try something risky that could fail, the computer rolls
a hidden 20-sided die against a difficulty number. Trying to
pick a lock, sneak past a guard, talk your way out of trouble,
or tell a convincing lie - all of these get a real roll.

The storyteller only sees the result and has to obey it. If the
lockpick fails, the lock holds. The AI cannot grant you a
success you didn't earn.

COMBAT
When a fight breaks out, wounds are decided by who's attacking
you. A lowly thug can't hurt you much. A legendary warrior
genuinely can take you down. There's no plot armor. On a bad
enough failure, you can go down for real.

DISGUISES
Wearing a stolen guard uniform? The computer decides if it
actually fools anyone:

- Ordinary soldiers who see nothing odd will wave you past
  (the classic confident walk-by).
- Captains and bosses will scrutinize you by default.
- Act nervous or suspicious, and your disguise gets questioned
  on the spot.

THE WORLD WON'T GO EASY ON YOU
There's a standing rule baked into everything: the world does
not owe you success. Characters have their own goals. You can
be turned away, get hurt, or lose. The world isn't trying to
make you feel good. It's trying to be true.

Also, the world doesn't scale down to match you. A tough guard
is still tough, no matter how strong you've gotten. Reality
stays real.

## 5. Time and Pacing

A REAL CLOCK
The story has a real clock that moves forward when meaningful
time passes. A new day, a long rest, a journey - these advance
the time. When a full day passes, the world updates itself.

THREE "MOODS" FOR EACH SCENE
Every moment is sorted into one of three moods, and the mood
shapes how everything feels:

DOWNTIME
- Calm, slower storytelling.
- Tricky tasks get a little easier.
- The world ticks forward every hour.

EXPLORATION
- The default mood for looking around and moving.
- The world ticks forward every 4 hours.

COMBAT
- Punchy, fast, intense storytelling.
- Tricky tasks get harder.
- Time basically freezes - the fight owns the moment.

## 6. The Map and Travel

THE WORLD IS A MAP OF CONNECTED PLACES
The world is made of named locations connected by paths, with a
marker showing where you are right now. For example:

The Rusty Lantern Tavern connects to:
- The Cellar
- The Market Square

The Market Square connects to:
- The North Road

And so on. You can only travel along paths that actually exist.

YOU CAN'T TELEPORT
When you try to go somewhere, the computer checks the map:

- If the place is right next to you, you move there.
- If it's not connected, you're told you can't go that way,
  and you're shown where you CAN go.
- If the place doesn't exist at all, you're told it's not real.

No walking through walls. No skipping across the map. The
geography is honest.

## 7. Weather

A SINGLE LIVING SKY
The world has one shared sky that changes over time. Weather
shifts in two ways:

- Sometimes the story directly calls for a change (a sudden
  storm rolls in).
- Otherwise, the weather drifts on its own as time passes. The
  longer it's been the same, the more likely it is to stay. But
  eventually it changes, and a change is always visible (it
  never "changes" to the exact same weather).

Weather stays steady during a fight. And when you're indoors,
the weather isn't mentioned at all - you don't care about the
rain when you're safe in the cellar.

## 8. Rumors and Gossip

WORDS TRAVEL BETWEEN PLACES
A rumor is just a juicy phrase - "the stranger paid in gold
coins" - and it spreads from place to place as time passes.

- Each rumor keeps track of which places have heard it.
- You only hear the rumors that YOUR current location knows.
  (The tavern has heard X. Not "Marcus specifically knows X.")
- Spread is messy and natural. Fresh rumors travel fast. Old
  rumors slow down. You can race ahead of a rumor, or sneak
  around to places it hasn't reached yet.

YOUR REPUTATION ISN'T A NUMBER
There's no "reputation score" or "faction standing meter." Your
reputation is simply the collection of stories people tell
about you. If the rumors say you're generous, people think
you're generous. If they say you're a thief, well.

## 9. Gear, Disguises, and Status

STUFF IS FLAVOR, NOT MATH
Your gear and disguises are described in words, never in
numbers. There is:

- No "Perception" stat
- No "Disguise Level"
- No "Guard Alertness Meter"

A disguise is just a description ("wearing a city guard's
uniform"), and the rules from section 4 decide if it holds.

BUFFS, DEBUFFS, AND DISGUISES
Status effects are quick descriptions with a time limit. Things
like:

- "Berserk Rage" (a boost, fades in 10 minutes)
- "Poisoned" (a penalty)
- "Guard Uniform" (a disguise)

The storyteller weaves these into the scene as flavor, never as
a spreadsheet.

QUEST FRUSTRATION
When a goal drags on too long, the mood around it sours. A stuck
quest slowly turns from hopeful, to frustrated, to desperate -
and the storytelling reflects that emotional weather.

## 10. Friends, Foes, and Busy NPCs

RELATIONSHIPS GROW SLOWLY AND FEEL REAL
Relationships aren't a number from -100 to +100. They're more
like stages:

Stranger, then Acquaintance, then Bonded friend
(or Nemesis, on the dark side)

Moving up a stage takes both time AND a real moment that earns
it. You can't rush a friendship in one chat. And betrayal works
the other way - it can ruin things instantly, no waiting.

OFF-SCREEN LIVES
Other characters have their own lives. The world quietly tracks
what they're up to when you're not around - "the blacksmith is
restocking the forge," for example. As time passes, these tasks
get resolved (sometimes they go wrong, and that becomes part of
the world).

## 11. Tools to Steer Your Story

When you want a nudge, WUPI has tools to help. All of them live
in the Wupi drawer (your in-game sidekick), so they never break
the feeling of the story on stage.

CROSSROADS ("GIVE ME IDEAS")
Stuck? Ask Wupi for options. She can offer choices from five
different angles - Action, Plot, Character, Explicit, or World.
Each angle gives you a few grounded ideas based on what's
actually happening in your story right now. Pick one and it
drops into your text box, ready to send.

GHOSTWRITER (TWO FLAVORS)

Flesh It Out
Give a rough idea of what you want to do, and Wupi will turn it
into vivid, in-character writing. You can review it before
sending, and undo if you don't like it.

Steer the Story
Drop a quiet hint about where you want things to go. The next
turn will quietly follow your hint, then forget it. It's a
one-time nudge, not a permanent rule.

## 12. Swiping, Rerolling, and Editing

You get a full set of chat tools to shape your story, all kept in
sync with the world.

SWIPEABLE VARIANTS (NEW)
When you don't love how the storyteller wrote a turn, you don't
lose the old version. Reroll it, and BOTH versions are kept side
by side. A little counter in the corner of the message shows
where you are, like  1/3 . Use the arrows to flip through every
version you generated and pick your favorite.

- Reroll adds a new version without throwing away the others.
- The arrows let you browse back through them anytime.
- The old way (one replace-on-reroll) is gone - nothing is lost.
- Flipping between versions does NOT change the world. Each
  version is just a different telling of the same turn, so the
  facts of what happened stay put no matter which you read.

DOUBLE-CLICK TO EDIT
Just double-click any message to edit it.

- Double-click YOUR OWN message to change what you said and
  branch the story from that point (the world rewinds to match).
- Double-click an AI's message to fix a typo or tweak the
  wording (just cosmetic - the world doesn't change).

The old little pencil button is gone. Double-click does it all.

REGENERATE A PIECE
Highlight any part of the AI's last message and ask for just that
piece to be rewritten. The new text flows smoothly into the words
before and after. No new turn, no change to the world.

ONE-CLICK UNDO
Every change to the world is quietly saved in a small history
(keeps the last 5). One click rolls back the last change and
shows you what shifted - for example, "Gold went from 100 to 80,
and Marcus moved from the tavern to the blacksmith."

## 13. Wupi, Your Helper

Wupi isn't just a mascot. She's the game-systems expert you can
talk to.

THE CODEX (HER LIBRARY)
Wupi has a library of lore, design notes, and how-to docs. When
she writes new lore, it's available instantly - no restart
needed. It shows up exactly when it's relevant, and costs
nothing when it isn't.

SHE CAN DO THINGS
Wupi can read, write, and organize files. She can create and
edit character cards, update your profile, and add to her
library. She does this through her own toolset, with safety
rules that protect the important engine files from being
touched.

HER PLAYBOOK
When WUPI starts up, Wupi loads her design playbook - her
standing notes on how stories and characters should be built.
It's there when she needs it, invisible when she doesn't.

## 14. What's New: Phase 5 (NPCs and Pictures)

This is the update currently in progress. It has two halves.

THE PROBLEM IT FIXES: THE "TELEPORTING NPC"
Before Phase 5, the world knew which general area you were in,
but not exactly who was standing in front of you. So if a
character stormed out of the tavern, the storyteller would
often forget a few turns later and have them chime in from the
corner. Characters would "teleport" back into scenes they'd
left. Phase 5 fixes this.

PART A - WHO'S ACTUALLY IN THE ROOM (DONE)
This part is finished and tested.

Now, every turn, WUPI quietly makes a list of who is physically
in the scene with you. The storyteller can ONLY see and use the
characters on that list.

- If a character isn't on the list, they cannot appear, speak,
  or act. They're simply not there.
- Characters are forgiving about one missed turn (in case the
  system hiccups), but after that, they're gone for good until
  they properly return.
- Once someone leaves and drops off the list, the storyteller
  is told they're gone and can't just summon them back.

This is much stronger than a rule saying "don't bring back
characters who left." Instead, the storyteller literally cannot
see characters who aren't there.

PART B - SCENE PICTURES (COMING SOON)
This part is still being built.

The goal is to generate a picture of each scene that matches
the exact moment you're in. WUPI builds a description of the
scene from three layers:

- WHERE you are (the look of the place).
- THE ENVIRONMENT (the weather and time of day).
- WHO'S THERE (the characters present and what they're doing).

Then that description will be turned into an actual picture.

Here's the lovely part: because the picture is built from the
SAME list of who's present as the storyteller uses, the picture
and the words can never disagree about who's in the room. If
someone isn't there in the story, they won't be in the picture
either.

Status: Part A is finished and tested. Part B (the picture
generator) is still being built.

## The Ideas Behind It All

A few simple beliefs shape everything above:

- The computer handles the rules. The AI handles the words.

- The world does not revolve around you.

- It's better to show who IS there than to say "don't add
  people who aren't."

- No spreadsheets in the storytelling. Numbers are only for
  time and dice. Everything else is a living description.

- The world has its own life. It changes, drifts, gossips, and
  forgets, whether you're watching or not.

- Each part of the world has exactly one boss. Time, weather,
  location, and who's present each have one clear authority,
  so they can never argue with themselves.
